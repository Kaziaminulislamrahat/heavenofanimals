Don’t Chase, Wait for Your Chance

Huntsman spiders are fast and good at hiding. Trying to trap them quickly often sends them running into a gap or behind furniture. I once watched a neighbor climb onto his kitchen counter with an ice cream tub, trying to box in a huntsman near the cabinets. The spider darted behind the fridge and vanished for days.

If you want to remove them safely (for both you and the spider), patience works best. Wait until the spider is sitting somewhere reachable and away from tight corners or clutter. If it’s out of reach, keep an eye on it from a distance; huntsmans often move lower as night falls.

Choose Tools That Match the Situation

Where the spider is makes all the difference:

  • On Walls or Ceilings: Use a lightweight plastic container (not glass, it’s heavy and slippery). If you can’t reach safely, tape your container to an extension pole or broom handle so you don’t have to balance on chairs.
  • In Tight Corners: Don’t poke with anything sharp. Instead, use a broom or long object with soft bristles to gently nudge the spider out into open space.
  • Behind Furniture: Sometimes it’s best not to move heavy objects at all, wait for the spider to come out rather than risk hurting yourself or squashing its legs.

Trying to act too quickly usually backfires: you either lose track of the spider, damage something in your home, or injure the animal.

Know When It’s Better Not to DIY

If seeing spiders makes your hands shake or heart race so much you can’t focus, ask someone else to help. It’s not worth risking a fall off a chair (or worse) just because you’re trying to do it alone. For spiders high above stairs or deep inside storage, wait until they come down before attempting removal.

Prevention: Entry Points Matter More Than Clutter

It’s tempting to believe that cleaning up will keep spiders away, but huntsman spiders enter even tidy homes if there are gaps around doors and windows. After several frustrating encounters in my own place, sealing cracks and fixing window screens made more difference than clearing out boxes ever did.

If you see huntsman spiders mostly after rain or when insects swarm outside lights, start by checking for gaps around doors, windows, vents, and screens. Only turn to decluttering if you find spiders deep in storage areas where they can hide undisturbed for months.

Skip Sprays, They Rarely Solve Anything

Chemical sprays may seem like an easy fix but rarely kill adult huntsman spiders outright. In one composite example: after spraying his basement heavily in hopes of getting rid of spiders, my neighbor ended up with even more upstairs as insects (their prey) tried escaping treated rooms. Sprays also pose risks for pets and children.

Focus on blocking entry points and reducing insect food sources instead of chemicals that scatter pests throughout your house.

Each Removal Makes It Easier Next Time

The first few removals are nerve-wracking, but it gets easier as you realize nothing terrible happens, just another routine problem solved. A friend who used to panic now calmly removes huntsmans using her dedicated “spider kit,” which she keeps under her sink for emergencies.

Quick Checklist: Be Ready Before You Need To

Assembling supplies ahead of time means less scrambling when surprised:

Spider-Removal Kit

  • Well-fitting gloves
  • Wide plastic container (clear is best)
  • Stiff cardboard or sturdy paper
  • Tape (to secure container if needed)
  • Extendable handle (broomstick plus tape works)

Keep your kit somewhere easy to grab, under the kitchen sink is ideal.

By waiting instead of chasing, using tools suited to each situation, sealing entry points before worrying about clutter, and avoiding sprays that only make matters worse, you’ll handle every encounter more smoothly, and with less stress for everyone involved. Every successful removal builds confidence for next time.
